Japan and Whistler are the "finding what you love" chapters: proof that not knowing at 18 is normal. They support the story; the brand stays about building stores, so they show up as one episode and as callbacks, not a series.

**Why I'm sharing this.** Social media makes everyone feel behind. I've done a lot, and I still feel behind. So this isn't a "made it" account. It's the journey in real time, for the people still grinding who don't have it all figured out.

**My belief, the thread through every story:**

- Your 20s are for finding what you love to do.
- Your 30s are for getting really good at it.
- Your 40s are for capitalizing on it.

This account is that process, happening in real time.

Story rules:

- One chapter per video, never the whole story at once.
- Specific moments over big lessons: a day, a place, a line someone said.
- Laugh at the struggle. No rags-to-riches, no income flexing.
- My current employer stays unnamed: "the stores I work on".
- Every story ends with what it means for the viewer and their store.

### 3. Who I serve

The viewer is a person, not a segment. Confirmed:

- **Primary:** a Shopify store owner doing roughly $5K to $100K a month, running it alone or with 1 to 5 people, wearing every hat.
- **Secondary:** someone launching their first store who wants to skip the beginner mistakes.
- **Not for:** enterprise brands with in-house teams, or get-rich-quick dropshippers.

### 7. My voice

### How every script is built

1. **Hook in three layers** in the first 3 seconds: what they see, read and hear.
2. **Seven hook types per topic:** Shock, Comprehensive, Common Mistake, Comparison, Question, Negative, Tutorial. One topic = up to 7 videos.
3. **One of 11 structures.** Educational structures drive comments (leads); stories drive shares (reach).
4. **Bullet script**, one sentence per step, plain words. AI drafts; I approve the voice.
5. **Caption does a different job** from the video: CTA, fine print or series counter.
6. **Broad hook, narrow value, niche CTA**, so the people who act are store owners.

### CTA ladder

| Level | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Bad | "Follow me!" |
| Good | "Follow for more Shopify fixes." |
| Great | "Want the 10 fixes I'd make on any Shopify store this week? Comment CRO and I'll send you my checklist." + proof on screen |

Never "link in bio". Keywords stay at 5 to 7 letters or fewer, one per lead magnet.

### What counts as a winner

A video with 5x my average reach. Winners get re-filmed in new formats (whiteboard, two-person, voiceover over B-roll) and noun-swapped for other store types.

## Weekly loop and build order

One 4 to 6 hour shoot day a week keeps a 5 to 7 video buffer, so one video goes out every day.

### Weekly loop

| When | What happens | Kevin's time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every morning | Claude scans for news, pain points and outlier videos; they all land in Ideas | 0 |
| Same day, as needed | Claude writes bullets for a news video; 10-minute phone shoot | 10 to 15 min |
| Sunday | Claude reviews Ideas and last week's stats, then writes about 10 scripts | 20 min to approve |
| Saturday (or a weekday) | Batch shoot. Say the video ID at the start of every take. Re-film last week's winners in a new format. | 4 to 6 hrs |
| After the shoot | Footage goes to Drive. Pipeline edits, captions and marks each row Ready for review. | 0 |
| Daily | Approve each video on my phone; it's scheduled to all 4 platforms | 1 min per video |
| Friday | Stats come back to the Sheet; Claude writes a what-worked note | 5 min to read |

### Build order for Claude Code

1. Brand Profile (this doc)
